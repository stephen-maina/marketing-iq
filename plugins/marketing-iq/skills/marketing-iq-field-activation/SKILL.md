# Activation procedure

Goal: deliver actual audience-specific copy while keeping unknowns visible.
Trigger: a Field brief or a clarification to the current activation activity.

1. In `direction-basis`, attribute the direction to the current user or the
   discovered accepted record, accurately identifying provenance. Separate source-supported
   product facts from proposed discussion topics.
   Apply the direction's rationale, not just its activity label: the invitation
   opening explains the attendee's practical reason to attend; the offer makes
   the intended takeaway and its use clear; the CTA gives a matching next step.
   Keep those choices consistent through all three points and the follow-up.
   A takeaway absent from direction may be proposed, not attributed to the
   record or promised as an approved deliverable. Do not ask for sensitive
   customer/case data; use fictional or non-sensitive examples where relevant.
2. In `invitation`, write a subject, body and one actionable CTA. Preserve known
   audience/activity details. Use placeholders only for unknown relevant
   logistics, such as `[date]`, `[time]`, `[registration link]` or `[sender]`.
   A scheduling invitation need not imply an already-booked event.
   Apply supplied tone, language, brand terminology and channel/length limits
   consistently across the kit. Do not invent a brand policy or ask for one
   when it would not materially affect this internal draft.
   If direction asks attendees to supply a preparation input, carry that request
   and its input restrictions into the CTA; generic registration or availability
   alone does not fulfill it. If no preparation input is specified, a relevant
   interest/scheduling CTA is sufficient; do not invent a prerequisite to attend.
   Establish the intended sector, role and geographic scope in the invitation,
   using a faithful paraphrase if clearer. Preserve that meaning through the
   kit; a later neutral "your team" need not repeat the entire audience label.
   Do not substitute another sector, role or region. Frame unevidenced audience
   difficulties as a question or conditional reason to attend, not a diagnosis.
3. In `talk-track`, write exactly three numbered points. Make each useful for the
   supplied audience: proposed need/question, bounded evidence-led discussion,
   and proposed next action. Do not imply product availability from a persona,
   promise ROI or import an internal customer example into outward copy.
4. In `follow-up`, write usable copy with a clear next action. With unknown
   history use a neutral interest/scheduling question, not an invented recap
   or denial of past contact. Explicit attendance permits an acknowledgement,
   not invented topics or agreements. Apply the Field reference's history rules.
5. In `publication-gaps`, distinguish evidence gaps, missing logistics and
   required human review/use permissions. Suggest a relevant measurement
   (for example, interest in a qualified follow-up), labelled as a proposal
   with no invented numerical target, historical result or guaranteed outcome.
   State how an owner could manually observe that signal. Actual marketer edits
   or outcome feedback, when supplied, remain attributed observations rather
   than proof of causation; no automatic analytics collection is implied.
6. Apply shared uncertainty/reassessment guidance. If audience, activity, timing
   or direction changes, revise every affected section, CTA and placeholder;
   remove contradicted old framing. Keep unrelated supported facts unless the
   change invalidates their applicability. Do not request unchanged details again.
   Apply decision-tools' scope-transition rules first: changed business scope
   or activity rechecks saved direction; logistics alone does not. A specific
   new topic must not be collapsed into a broad old topic just to match a record.
   New tasks and explicit returns to earlier tasks have separate scope/ledger
   handling under the shared contract. Same folder or same four keys alone
   does not approve a different objective.
   For a narrow wording request, preserve unaffected text exactly and change
   only the requested part plus genuinely dependent corrections. Explain any
   necessary factual correction; submit the whole revised pair for fresh review.

EXCLUDE: direction inherited from unaccepted chat, treating unknown history as no history, factual
claims from metadata, invented approvals, automatic distribution, internal
customer disclosures, and headings/placeholders instead of useful supported copy.

# Validation gates and rule catalog

- **FA-WF-01 Intake:** current explicit direction or a selected applicable accepted
  record, never a previous assistant proposal.
- **FA-WF-02 Utility:** subject/body/CTA, exactly three talk points and actual follow-up.
  The opening, useful takeaway and CTA apply the chosen rationale; repeating a
  takeaway's name without explaining its purpose is insufficient.
- **FA-WF-03 Reassessment:** changed input propagates to all dependent copy without
  silently changing evidence, permissions or direction approval state.
- **FA-WF-04 Publication:** every kit remains an internal draft. Missing external
  approval is a publication gap, not a blocker to supported internal drafting.
- **FA-WF-05 Isolation:** current scope and intended activity determine decision
  applicability, not chat recency or a shared source folder. Load matching CLI
  results only; no global decision/history scan.

# Worked examples (fictional)

The current user proposes a readiness briefing for operations leads; body
evidence supports readiness assessment, but no event date is supplied. Draft a
useful briefing invitation with unknown logistics marked, three proposed talk
points and a neutral scheduling follow-up. Do not block merely for missing dates.

The user changes the activity to a short discovery call for service owners.
Revise the audience and activity in invitation, talk points and follow-up,
while retaining the proposed approval state. Do not leave a workshop CTA.

No direction is supplied, but discovery returns one current accepted record:
use it and name its provenance; no repeat of the prior chat is needed.
If its reason is to clarify prerequisites before choosing a workflow, offer a
questions checklist and a discovery next step, not an experiment already chosen.
If its reason is to examine one preparation step, offer a bounded working output
that helps choose the next action, not a generic product demonstration.
Two applicable alternatives require a user choice, not a newest-file heuristic.
If discovery finds no applicable record, request a proposed direction. A prior
unaccepted Launch recommendation is not a substitute.

"Make it 15 minutes" retains current direction and spent evidence allowances;
the changed copy gets its own shared bounded answer-review cycle.
"New task: healthcare operations in Europe" does not inherit a government
decision, source folder or audience. Changing an existing invitation to citizen
services rechecks scope without resetting its budget. Requesting a production
launch cannot turn an accepted discovery-only record into launch approval.

Audience contrast: "leaders running government services around the world" can
preserve worldwide government scope, and a later "your team" can refer to them.
Changing the invitation to "US private-sector hospital executives" cannot:
correct direction-basis metadata does not repair contradictory audience copy.

pre-dispatch checks and inline-evidence handoff before invoking the reviewer.

# Final check

Run the shared final check. Verify the supplied/discovered direction, all five sections,
three numbered talk points, known audience details and all changed dependencies.
Check that the opening, offer and CTA apply the rationale and preserve its
boundaries, not merely repeat an example phrase.
Unknown logistics/history stay unknown, not invented. No implicit approval,
cross-session private cache or loss of source restrictions. Preserve the
canonical JSON; render its draft-copy text verbatim in the shared readable view.
Blocked responses keep sections and facts empty and do not render a kit.
